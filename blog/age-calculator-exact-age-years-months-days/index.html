Age Calculator: How to Calculate Your Exact Age in Years, Months and Days

October 5, 20265 min read

An age calculator tells you exactly how old someone is, based on a date of birth and a second date, usually today. Instead of giving a rough answer like "25 years old", it breaks the result into years, months and days, and often into total weeks, days and hours lived. This guide explains how the calculation works, where it is useful, and how to avoid common mistakes.

What Is an Age Calculator?

An age calculator is a simple online tool that compares two dates: the date of birth and the "age as on" date. The difference between them is your age. Most people use today's date, but you can also pick any past or future date to see how old a person was or will be.

A good age calculator shows:

  • Exact age in years, months and days
  • Total months, weeks and days lived
  • Total hours and minutes lived
  • Days remaining until the next birthday
  • Day of the week you were born

How to Calculate Age Manually

You can work out your age by hand in three steps:

  1. Subtract the years. Take the current year and subtract the birth year.
  2. Check the birthday. If your birthday has not happened yet this year, subtract one year from the result.
  3. Count the leftover months and days. Count from your last birthday to today to get the extra months and days.

Example

Date of birth: 15 March 2000. Age as on: 5 October 2026.

  • Years: 2026 - 2000 = 26 (the birthday has already passed in 2026)
  • Months: from 15 March to 15 September = 6 months
  • Days: from 15 September to 5 October = 20 days

Exact age: 26 years, 6 months and 20 days. Doing this by hand gets tricky when months have different lengths, which is why a calculator is faster and more reliable.

Calculate Age on a Specific Date

Many forms ask for your age as on a particular cutoff date, not your age today. This is common for:

  • Job applications with a minimum or maximum age limit
  • School and college admissions
  • Exam eligibility and competition age groups
  • Insurance and pension planning

Simply set the "age as on" field to the cutoff date mentioned in the official circular or notice. Always confirm the cutoff date and the rules directly with the organization or institution, because they can change by program and by year.

How Leap Years Affect Age

A leap year adds an extra day, 29 February, every four years. This matters for two reasons:

  • The total number of days you have lived includes every leap day since your birth.
  • If you were born on 29 February, your birthday only appears in leap years. In other years, people usually celebrate on 28 February or 1 March, and the rule used for official purposes can vary.

A reliable age calculator handles these cases automatically so you do not have to count extra days yourself.

Age Difference Between Two People

The same method works for comparing two birth dates. Enter both dates and the calculator gives the gap in years, months and days. People use this to check the age gap between siblings, couples, or a parent and child.

Chronological Age for Children and Babies

For babies and young children, age is often measured in months or weeks rather than years. Chronological age means the time since birth, and it is used in school admission checks, developmental screenings and growth tracking. For example, a child who is 30 months old is 2 years and 6 months old. If you need advice about a baby's development or a premature birth, speak with a doctor, as they may use a corrected age.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Ignoring the cutoff date. Your age today may differ from your age on the date a form requires.
  • Counting only the year. Subtracting birth year from current year can be off by one if your birthday has not yet passed.
  • Forgetting month lengths. Months have 28 to 31 days, so simple division by 30 gives inaccurate results.
  • Mixing date formats. Check whether you are entering day/month/year or month/day/year.
