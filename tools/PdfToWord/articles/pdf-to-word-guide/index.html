Converting a PDF to Word means turning a fixed-layout document into an editable .docx file, so you can change text, fix typos, reuse content or update a document when you no longer have the original.

Why Convert PDF to Word?

  • Edit text that is locked inside a PDF.
  • Reuse content in new documents, emails or reports.
  • Update old contracts, CVs and forms.
  • Share an editable copy with a colleague.

How to Convert PDF to Word in 3 Steps

  1. Add your PDF. Click the upload area in the converter above or drag in one or more .pdf files.
  2. Choose settings. Keep the original page size or pick A4 or US Letter, and decide how scanned pages are handled.
  3. Convert and download. Press Convert to Word, then download each .docx or all of them as a ZIP.

What Is Kept and What May Need Touch-Ups

ConvertKit Lite reads the real text in your PDF and writes it as normal Word paragraphs. Bold, italic, font sizes, paragraph breaks, indents and page breaks are kept. Complex tables, multi-column layouts and embedded graphics may need small touch-ups in Word.

What About Scanned PDFs?

A scanned PDF contains pictures of pages, not text, so there is nothing to edit. The converter inserts those pages as images by default, or skips them. OCR is not included, so if you need editable text from a scan, run it through an OCR tool first.

Tips for Cleaner Results

  • Start from the original PDF rather than a scan or screenshot.
  • Remove any password first. Protected PDFs are not supported.
  • Proofread the result and adjust fonts if needed.
  • Convert several files at once and download them as a ZIP.

With ConvertKit Lite the conversion runs in your browser, so your files stay on your device and are not uploaded. Need to go the other way? Use our Word to PDF converter, or read what a DOCX file is.

Ways to Convert PDF to Word

There is more than one way to turn a PDF into an editable document, and each has trade-offs.

  • Browser-based converter (like the one above): no install, and with ConvertKit Lite your file is processed on your own device. Best for text-based PDFs.
  • Microsoft Word: Word can open a PDF and rebuild it as an editable document. It works offline, but you need Word and the layout can shift.
  • Google Docs: upload the PDF to Google Drive and open it with Google Docs. Handy if you already use Google, but your file goes through Google's servers.
  • Desktop PDF editors: full-featured and often paid. Worth it for complex layouts you convert every day.

Which should you pick? For a quick, private conversion of a normal text PDF, a browser-based tool is the fastest route. For heavy tables or scans, expect some manual cleanup whichever method you use.

Why Does Formatting Change After Converting?

A PDF does not store paragraphs the way Word does. It stores individual pieces of text placed at exact positions on the page. A converter has to work out where lines end, which lines belong to the same paragraph, and which fonts to use. That is why a conversion is never a perfect copy, and why fonts that are not installed on your computer get replaced by similar ones.

Common Problems and How to Fix Them

  • The Word file contains only pictures: the PDF is a scan. Use an OCR tool first, or keep the page images.
  • Lines break in the wrong place: turn on paragraph marks (¶) in Word and delete stray line breaks.
  • Fonts look different: install the original font, or select all text and apply one you prefer.
  • The file will not convert: check whether the PDF is password protected and remove the password first.
  • Tables look messy: rebuild the table in Word or paste the data into a new table.

Best Practices for Work and Study Documents

  • Keep the original PDF. For signed contracts it stays the official copy.
  • Proofread names, numbers and dates after converting.
  • Compare the page count and headings with the original.
  • Save the edited file under a new name.
  • Want several PDFs in one Word file? Merge the PDFs first, then convert.

Will the formatting stay exactly the same?

Text, bold, italic, font sizes, paragraphs and page breaks are kept. Complex tables, columns and graphics may need small fixes in Word.

Can I edit a scanned PDF?

A scan is a picture of text, so it cannot be edited directly. The tool can insert scanned pages as images, but it does not include OCR.

Can I convert PDF to Word without Microsoft Word?

Yes. The converter creates a standard .docx file that also opens in Google Docs, LibreOffice and Apple Pages.

Are my PDF files uploaded to a server?

No. The conversion runs in your browser, so your files stay on your device.

Is there a file size limit?
