Why convert MP3 to WAV?

MP3 is great for storing and sharing music in small files, but it is not always the right format to work with. WAV holds the sound as raw, uncompressed audio, which many tools and devices prefer.

  • Edit without extra quality loss. Editing an MP3 and saving it again as MP3 compresses it a second time. Working from a WAV avoids that repeated loss until you export your final version.
  • Use it in audio and video software. Audio editors, DAWs, video editors and sampling tools all accept WAV, and some work more smoothly with it than with MP3.
  • Meet a format requirement. Some devices, game engines, phone systems, speech tools and school or contest submissions ask for WAV files specifically.
  • Choose your sample rate and channels. Set a specific sample rate or mix down to mono when another program needs it.
  • Keep your audio private. The conversion happens in your browser, so recordings, voice notes and unreleased tracks are never uploaded to a server.

How this tool creates your WAV

ConvertKit Lite uses your browser's built-in audio engine to decode each MP3 into raw sound, then writes that sound into a standard WAV file.

  • 16-bit PCM output. The result is a standard 16-bit PCM WAV, the most widely supported WAV format.
  • Original sample rate by default. Keeping the original avoids unnecessary resampling. You can also pick 48,000, 44,100, 22,050 or 16,000 Hz.
  • Optional mono. Choose Mono to mix left and right into one channel and cut the file size in half.
  • No quality is added or invented. WAV stores what the MP3 contains. It will not sound better than the original MP3, but it will not lose anything further.
  • Audio only. Tags such as title, artist and album art are not included in the WAV.

How big will the WAV file be?

WAV size depends only on the sample rate, the number of channels and the length, not on the original MP3. Here is roughly how much space one minute of audio takes at each setting:

SettingApprox. size per minute
48,000 Hz, stereoabout 11 MB
44,100 Hz, stereoabout 10 MB
44,100 Hz, monoabout 5 MB
22,050 Hz, stereoabout 5 MB
16,000 Hz, monoabout 1.8 MB

For comparison, a typical 128 kbps MP3 is about 1 MB per minute, so a CD-quality stereo WAV is around ten times larger. Sizes are approximate.

Tips for the best result

  • Keep the sample rate on Original unless a program or device needs a specific one. It gives the most faithful result.
  • Use Mono for voice. Podcasts, lectures, meetings and voice notes sound the same in mono and take half the space. For speech-only use, 16,000 Hz mono is smaller still.
  • Do not raise the sample rate to improve quality. Choosing 48,000 Hz for a 44,100 Hz MP3 makes the file larger without adding detail.
  • Convert long files a few at a time. Decoding happens in your browser's memory, so very long recordings or big batches can be slow on phones and older computers.
  • Need to keep tags or cover art? WAV from this tool is audio only. Keep your original MP3 as the master copy if you want its tags.
  • Start from the best source. If you still have the original recording or a higher-bitrate file, convert that instead. A higher-quality MP3 gives a better WAV.
  • Check your free space. Make sure you have room for the larger files, especially when downloading many at once as a ZIP.

Frequently asked questions

Quick answers about the ConvertKit Lite MP3 to WAV tool.

Are my audio files uploaded to a server?

No. The conversion runs in your browser, so your files stay on your device.

Will the sound quality improve?

No. MP3 is lossy, so detail removed during MP3 encoding cannot be brought back. WAV simply stores the decoded sound without further loss, which is useful for editing software and devices that need WAV.

Why is the WAV file so much bigger?

WAV stores raw, uncompressed audio. A 3 MB MP3 can become a WAV of 30 MB or more.

Which sample rate should I choose?

Keep Original unless you need a specific rate. Lower rates such as 16,000 Hz make smaller files and suit speech, but they cut high frequencies.

Can I convert many files at once?

Yes. Add several MP3 files, convert them together, and download each WAV or one ZIP. Very long files use a lot of memory, so convert those a few at a time.

Are tags and cover art kept?

No. The WAV contains the audio only, without ID3 tags or album art.

What WAV format does this converter create?

It creates standard 16-bit PCM WAV files, the most widely supported WAV format. They open in audio editors, video editors, media players and most devices that accept WAV.

What does the Mono option do?

Mono mixes the left and right channels into a single channel. This halves the WAV file size and suits voice recordings, podcasts, lectures and phone calls, but you lose stereo separation.

Is converting MP3 to WAV lossless?

The WAV stores the decoded MP3 audio with no further quality loss. It cannot bring back detail the MP3 already lost, so it will not sound better than the MP3 you started with.

Is there a limit on file length?

There is no upload limit because nothing is uploaded, but long files need a lot of memory in your browser. Standard WAV files also top out at about 4 GB, which is roughly 6 hours of CD-quality stereo audio.

Does it work on phones and tablets?

Yes, in modern browsers that support audio decoding, including Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari on Android, iPhone, iPad, Windows, Mac and Linux. Large files may be slow on older phones.

Is the MP3 to WAV converter really free?

Yes. It is free with no account, no watermark and no limit on how many files you convert.

Can I convert MP3 to WAV on an iPhone without an app?

Yes, in a modern mobile browser that supports audio decoding. Choose the file with the file picker and convert.

Why is a long file slow on my phone?

The conversion happens on your phone and uses a lot of memory. Try a computer, or convert shorter files.
